LC Circuit Resonance with Arduino

This project demonstrates how an Arduino can excite a simple LC circuit and reveal its resonance behavior using timed charge pulses. The experiment focuses on analog signal dynamics, digital control, and the interaction between software timing and circuit physics.

Learning goals:
  • Observe charge and discharge processes in an LC circuit
  • Understand resonance and how excitation timing affects amplitude
  • Use Arduino to generate timed pulses for stimulating analog circuits
  • Analyze transient analog signals with Tinkercad simulation

Experimental Setup

  • Arduino digital pin: generates short HIGH pulses to charge the LC circuit
  • Inductor (L) and Capacitor (C): form the LC tank
  • NPN transistor: switches current into the LC tank
  • Oscilloscope probe: connected across L and C to observe voltage oscillations

Measurement Principle

The Arduino sends short charge pulses to the LC circuit with a defined time delay. When the pulse period is close to half of the natural LC period, the circuit resonates, producing large sinusoidal voltage oscillations: 

T₀ = 2 π √(L C)    (full oscillation period)
f₀ = 1 / T₀        (resonance frequency)
Optimal delay ≈ T₀ / 2

By varying the pulse delay, the oscillation amplitude can be maximized (resonance) or reduced.

Observed Behavior

  • Voltage across the LC tank oscillates sinusoidally after each pulse
  • Maximum amplitude occurs when excitation delay matches resonance condition
  • Amplitude decays over time due to circuit damping
  • Too short or too long delays result in smaller oscillations

Simulation

The project can be fully simulated using Tinkercad Circuits. Adjust L, C, or pulse delay and observe the resonance effect live on the oscilloscope.

Technical Notes

  • Arduino timing is controlled in microseconds using delayMicroseconds()
  • Oscilloscope probe measures analog voltage across L and C
  • Transient signal can be sampled via analog input, but fast oscillations may exceed Arduino ADC resolution
  • Simulation approximates ideal LC behavior with damping

Limitations

  • Arduino ADC is too slow to fully capture high-frequency LC oscillations
  • Oscillation amplitude depends on pulse timing and component tolerances
  • Simulation is idealized; real circuits have parasitic resistance and inductance

Didactic Intent

This experiment illustrates:

  • Interaction between digital pulse generation and analog circuit response
  • Concept of resonance and energy transfer in LC circuits
  • Challenges of measuring fast analog signals with microcontrollers
  • Observation of physics principles via simulation before real hardware implementation
